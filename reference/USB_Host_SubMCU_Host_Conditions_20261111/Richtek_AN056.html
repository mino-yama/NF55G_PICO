the pinning is laid out in such a way that power connections will always meet correctly. Since the receptacle has two rows of D+ and D- where the two D+ lines and the two D- lines are connected together so the D+ and D- will always meet-up. Therefore the USB Type-C CC lines are used to identify the cable orientation and will configure the TX/RX hardware to work correctly. </li> </ul> <p class="MsoNormal">Figure 1 : USB Type-C receptacle and cable plug pinning. </p> <p class="MsoNormal"> <a href="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image013_en.jpg" class="fancybox"> <img width="603" height="293" width="603" height="293" id="Picture 74" alt="Technical Document Image Preview" src="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image013_en.jpg" /></a></p> <p class="MsoCaption">Figure 1</p> <ul> <li>Another difference between older USB standards and the new USB Type-C standard is the dual role capability : USB Type-C cables have identical plugs on either side of the cable, which means that devices connected together have to tell each other who will be acting as host and who will be acting as device. Note that these roles need to be determined for both data and power after connection of the cable: For data transmission, the host is called the Downstream Facing Port (DFP) and the device is called the Upstream Facing Port (UFP). For power, the power provider is the Source and power consumer is the Sink. Some applications can have Dual Roles for Data (DRD) and Dual Role for Power (DRP). The CC lines will be used to define the power role of the two connected devices during connection. </li> <br /> <br /> <li>Another advantage of the USB Type-C system is its higher power capability. Whereas the legacy USB was able to provide 2.5W only, USB Type-C is able to provide up to 5V/3A, 15W max, and with Power Delivery, the voltage &amp; current range is increased to 20V/5A for maximum power of 100W. This makes it possible to power larger devices from the USB PD port, like monitors, and also allows charging devices with large battery packs like notebook computers. The new USB PD 3.0 standard also supports Programmable Power Supply (PPS) which allows fine adjustment of the bus voltage and current, and even allows lower voltage than 5V; PPS VBUS can be adjusted down to 3V which is very useful in Direct Charge systems where the Bus voltage is directly connected to the battery for highest efficiency charging. Standard type-C cables are rated for currents up to 3A. For higher currents up to 5A, special E-marked cables need to be used. These cables include a Cable ID IC that indentifies the cables capability via the CC line. E-mark ICs require a 5V power supply to be applied to the cable VCONN pin via one of the CC wires. </li> <br /> <br /> <li>The Super Speed + 10Gbps data rate via the TX/RX pairs makes it possible to transfer signals that previously needed dedicated cables, like HDMI/DisplayPort/Thunderbolt, and allows 4k video to be transferred over USB Type-C. HDMI signal transfer via USB Type-C cables requires special use of the data transfer pairs in the cable, which is called Alternate Mode. </li> </ul> <p class="MsoNormal">Below figures show some examples of USB Type-C applications and related Richtek power management ICs : </p> <p class="MsoNormal"> <a href="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image056_en.jpg" class="fancybox"> <img width="604" height="116" width="604" height="116" alt="Technical Document Image Preview" src="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image056_en.jpg" /></a></p> <p class="MsoCaption">Figure 2. </p> <p class="MsoNormal">Figure 2 shows a typical USB-PD adapter charging solution, where the adapter power supply provides an adjustable USB bus voltage 5V, 9V, 15V or 20V based on the request of the device attached.</p> <p class="MsoNormal"> <a href="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image015_en.jpg" class="fancybox"> <img width="604" height="132" width="604" height="132" alt="Technical Document Image Preview" src="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image015_en.jpg" /></a></p> <p class="MsoCaption">Figure 3</p> <p class="MsoNormal">Figure 3 is a similar application as Figure 2, but now utilizing the USBPD 3.0 Programmable Power Supply function to fine-adjust the bus voltage over a wider voltage range. How USB Type-C configures data and power </h2> <br> <h3>Data transfer</h3> <p class="MsoNoSpacing"> <a href="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image021_en.jpg" class="fancybox"> <img width="600" width="600" alt="Technical Document Image Preview" src="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image021_en.jpg" /></a></p> <p class="MsoCaption">Figure 9 : non-twisted, non-flipped straight connection. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">Figure 9 shows a non-twisted cable with un-flipped connector &ndash;receptacle position. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">From left receptacle to right receptacle, the RX1pair connects to TX1 pair and Rx2 pair connects to TX2 pair. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">D+ connects to D+ and D- connects to D-. SBU1 connects to SBU2. CC1 connects via CC wire to CC1. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">USB-3.1 uses only two data pairs. In this case, the high speed data travels via RX1+/- and TX1+/- from one side to the other side. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">Note that VCONN is not wired through; the VCONN voltage for powering cable E-mark ICs is only provided from one side, after determining whether a cable includes an E-mark IC or not. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing"> <a href="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image022_en.jpg" class="fancybox"> <img width="537" height="442" width="537" height="442" alt="Technical Document Image Preview" src="/content/dam/richtek-eds/resources/application-notes/an056/html-images-en/image022_en.jpg" /></a></p> <p class="MsoCaption">Figure 10 : twisted cable connection</p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">Figure 10 shows a twisted cable connection with receptacle in same position. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">In this condition, from left receptacle to right receptacle, the RX1 pair connects to TX2 pair and Rx2 pair connects to TX1 pair. D+ still connects to D+ and D- still connects to D-. SBU1 connects to SBU1, SBU2 to SBU2. CC1 connects via CC wire to CC2. High speed data now needs to travel from RX1+/- and TX1+/- left to RX2+/- and TX2+/- on the right. It means the data transceiver needs to switch to different data pairs. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">There are totally 4 connection possibilities: flipped or non flipped receptacle and twisted or non twisted cable. </p> <p class="MsoNoSpacing">In USB 3.1 systems, the RX/TX data-lines require a multiplexer to correctly reconfigure the RX/TX lines in all possible cable orientations.
